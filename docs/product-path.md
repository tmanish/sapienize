# Sapienize product path and status

Last updated: 2026-08-18

This is the durable product-direction log for Sapienize. Update it when a milestone changes status, a measurement claim becomes supportable, or a host adapter ships. It records product intent separately from current capability.

## North star

Sapienize helps people restore their human voice in assisted writing. Its primary product outcome is a faithful rewrite that:

1. preserves meaning, facts, claims, names, numbers, dates, URLs, quotations, negation, uncertainty, and scope;
2. matches measurable habits from the user's authentic writing when a representative sample is available;
3. otherwise follows a requested persona only for register and diction, without inventing identity, biography, credentials, anecdotes, facts, opinions, or first-person experience; and
4. reduces generic or model-associated stylistic patterns only where doing so does not conflict with semantic preservation or the authentic voice.

The product ambition is robust performance as models, domains, and detectors change. That ambition is not an accuracy claim. Sapienize will publish detector, voice, or semantic performance claims only when a representative, legally usable benchmark and leakage-safe held-out evaluation support them.

## Non-negotiable measurement boundary

Detectors and provenance are independent measurement surfaces, not rewrite objectives.

- External detector results may be recorded and compared during evaluation, with detector name, version when known, date, raw response, score semantics, threshold, calibration status, and limitations preserved.
- Detector or provenance results do not enter rewrite prompts, convergence criteria, candidate eligibility, or ranking.
- A style heuristic is an explainable, uncalibrated inventory of configured signals; it is not a probability or substitute for a trained detector.
- Voice similarity is descriptive and semantic verification is heuristic. Neither establishes identity or guarantees perfect preservation.
- Missing or unsupported provenance evidence means unknown, not absent.

This boundary lets the team measure detector behavior—including disagreement, false positives, and version drift—without training the rewrite loop to chase a mutable vendor score.

## Claude marks and the copy boundary

Text generated by Sapienize is generated by the selected host or provider. If Claude performs the rewrite, supported Claude output may carry an embedded text mark. Copying that output is not a removal step: a mark encoded through token choices can remain in the copied visible text, and another Claude regeneration may produce newly marked output.

Anthropic says public detection details are forthcoming. The current Sapienize code exposes an Anthropic provenance compatibility boundary, but no official Claude verifier is integrated; it therefore reports `unsupported` unless an official verifier is explicitly configured. Sapienize cannot currently determine that copied or regenerated text is watermark-free and must not label it that way. Document-integrity findings such as invisible or directional characters are reported separately and are not evidence for or against an embedded text mark.

Current reference: [Anthropic, “How Claude marks AI-generated content”](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content).

## Product-aligned next path

The right next move is data, not more heuristic rules. Work proceeds in this order.

### 1. Create the evaluation-data protocol

Add dataset manifests covering licensing, consent, collection method, edit history, domain, model/version, and leakage-safe train/evaluation splits.

Status: **foundation implemented on 2026-08-18.** `eval/manifest.js` now validates a separately versioned governance manifest, exact dataset binding, source rights, privacy handling, ordered lineage, frozen split membership, grouping leakage, and normalized exact-duplicate leakage. The committed manifest remains synthetic plumbing. Near-duplicate audit tooling and integration of manifest identity into evaluator reports remain work for the representative-benchmark phase.

Exit criteria:

- a versioned manifest schema and validator;
- documented inclusion, consent, retention, and deletion rules;
- author-, source-, and near-duplicate-aware split policy;
- dataset fingerprints and reproducible run configuration; and
- private voice material remains local and excluded from committed fixtures.

### 2. Assemble a representative benchmark

Cover genuine human writing, AI output, grammar-corrected writing, mixed documents, human-edited AI output, and voice-restored output across domains and lengths. Keep private voice material local.

Exit criteria:

- licensing and consent are traceable for every record;
- domains, document lengths, source types, models, and edit histories are sufficiently represented;
- evaluation reports author-held-out, domain, model, source-type, and length slices where sample sizes support them; and
- committed synthetic data remains identified as plumbing-only, not an accuracy benchmark.

### 3. Integrate one legitimate external detector

Use it strictly as a versioned evaluation observation. Preserve its raw response, score semantics, threshold, calibration status, and date; never feed it into rewrite ranking.

Exit criteria:

- access is terms-compliant and credentials are user/environment supplied;
- the adapter has deterministic mocks and makes no paid calls in default tests;
- vendor-specific meanings remain explicit rather than normalized into false equivalence; and
- unavailable or rate-limited observations remain unavailable, not zero.

### 4. Use benchmark failures to improve voice and semantic verification

Prioritize stronger named-entity and claim handling, genre-aware voice comparisons, author-held-out evaluation, and scalable matching for long documents.

Exit criteria:

- improvements are tied to reproducible failure cases;
- protected-content regressions remain candidate blockers;
- genre/register mismatch is surfaced as a limitation; and
- semantic integrity stays ahead of voice similarity and style in candidate ranking.

### 5. Consider a calibrated local detector only after sufficient data exists

Ship it only if held-out evaluation supports defensible performance and calibration claims.

Exit criteria:

- training and evaluation data are separated without author/source leakage;
- performance, calibration, false-positive behavior, limitations, and supported domains are documented;
- the model remains an analysis result, not a rewrite reward; and
- unsupported domains or insufficient evidence return an honest unavailable/limited state.

### 6. Implement MCP afterward

The interface is documented; implementation should follow once the core schemas and evaluation workflow have remained stable through real use.

Exit criteria:

- the server is a thin transport over the shared core;
- local operations remain local;
- provider credentials are configured outside logged tool arguments; and
- the MCP surface preserves every result boundary documented above.

## Cross-host `/sapienize` direction

Build one canonical, host-neutral Sapienize workflow around the shared core and schemas, then generate or package thin adapters for supported hosts.

Planned sequence:

1. remove host-specific assumptions from the canonical workflow while retaining the existing Claude package;
2. define host packaging and invocation for ChatGPT/Codex and Gemini based on each host's supported skill mechanism;
3. add host-specific conformance tests that verify the same semantic, voice, detector, and provenance boundaries; and
4. use MCP as a shared transport only after the data/evaluation and core contracts have stabilized.

The universal product concept is `/sapienize`; the literal command, package layout, availability, and permission model may differ by host. Until those adapters ship, documentation must say that the current generated `.skill` is Claude-oriented and that the CLI is the reusable cross-provider surface.

## Status log

### 2026-08-18 — Data-first product path adopted

- Restated voice restoration and semantic preservation as the primary product outcome.
- Kept detector and provenance systems as independent evaluation observations rather than rewrite targets.
- Established licensed, consent-aware benchmark work as the next milestone before detector performance claims.
- Recorded that copied Claude-generated text may preserve an embedded mark and that current Sapienize cannot certify watermark-free output.
- Set cross-host adapters as the delivery direction while retaining accurate status: Claude-oriented skill today, ChatGPT/Codex and Gemini packaging later, MCP after schema and evaluation stability.

### 2026-08-18 — Evaluation-data protocol foundation implemented

- Added a versioned, runtime-validated dataset governance manifest and bound the public synthetic fixture to its exact dataset fingerprint.
- Required evaluation rights, redistribution and attribution instructions, withdrawal handling, consent basis, collection method, privacy classification, ordered edit lineage, and provider/model/version metadata where AI participates.
- Enforced frozen exactly-once splits across author, model, lineage, and source-document groups, plus exact normalized duplicate detection across splits.
- Reserved `eval/.local/` for ignored private voice material, consent records, detector responses, and local reports.
- Kept the boundary explicit: this validates data governance and evaluator plumbing; it is not a representative benchmark and supports no detector-accuracy or voice-quality claim.
